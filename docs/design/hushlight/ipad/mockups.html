Hushlight · design draft · HL-058 · 2026-09-09

A stand, or a hand.

Nightstand was written about one object: a phone face-up on the nightstand at 3am. An iPad in that room is a different object — propped on a tray table and read across the room, or in the partner's hand, keeping the record. Today the app runs there in a phone-shaped window. This is what it looks like designed for the room instead.

Regular · Stand

Landscape is a stand.

Nobody is holding it, so the reading distance is a room's. The leading figure takes a new DesignKit role — .distance, about 132 pt — and the ledger goes quiet beside it. The button stays the whole lower edge of its pane: whoever does reach for it should not have to look.

HL-013 asked for this — “the nightstand is literal” — and got it through the Live Activity in StandBy. iPad has no StandBy. This is the same surface at full size, inside the app.

Regular · Hand

Portrait is a hand.

The partner's screen. They are not the one having contractions; they are keeping the record and reading it back to the nurse. For them the question is never the count alone — it is what the last hour has looked like. So History is not a screen they go to. It sits beside the timer, always.

Same two panes, the figure back at .hero. Guidance and the How-it-works card stay pushed screens and sheets in every regime: read once, occasionally, not worth a permanent pane.

Compact

Every width is a first-class width.

Split View at a third, Slide Over, a small Stage Manager window: the compact size class gets today's iPhone column, untouched — it already survives 320 pt. Between compact and regular the single column simply widens, with a maximum content width so a 700 pt line of prose never happens.

Regimes switch at the size-class boundary, continuously under Stage Manager. The HL-057 lesson applies: anything a regime switch would rebuild — a refusal, a sheet, a scroll position — is owned above the switch.

  • OrientationAll four on iPad. iPhone stays portrait (decision 0018)
  • Chosen bySize class and width — never device model

Regular · both

A keyboard on the tray table.

A Magic Keyboard is plausible and cheap to honour. Five shortcuts, surfaced by holding ⌘ — the system's own discoverability, so nothing new is drawn. Pointer hover on the capsules and the slab is the system effect.

SpaceStart, then End — the one button, on the biggest key
⌘ ZUndo the last touch
⌘ JHistory — ⌘H is iPadOS's own Home, ⌘1/⌘L/⌘Y are Simulator.app's
⌘ GGuidance
EscClose a sheet
The ratchet

The iPad keeps its own record.

No sync. Nothing a user times leaves the device, there is no iCloud entitlement and no network code — so an iPhone in the bedroom and an iPad on the stand are two instruments, not one. The design says so on Guidance rather than hiding it. For both roles above, that is enough: the partner's iPad is the record; the stand shows whichever device is timing.

The Live Activity already appears on the iPad Lock Screen (verified 2026-09-09). No Dynamic Island, no StandBy, no widget — iPhone surfaces and the App Group, respectively, and the app imitates neither.

Guidance · what this app is · one added sentence

This iPad keeps its own record. Nothing here is shared with your iPhone or with anyone.

Not decided here. A user-initiated hand-off — the For the call export as a file she AirDrops from one device and opens on the other — clears the ratchet's letter and edges its spirit. It is Christian's call under gate 3, filed as HL-058d, and this design assumes it is not built.
