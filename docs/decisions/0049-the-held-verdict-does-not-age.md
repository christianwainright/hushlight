# 0049 · The held verdict does not age

**Date:** 2026-08-26 · **Item:** HL-029 (F-0052) · **Status:** decided

## Context

The long-haul lens, walking the third night of prodromal labor, noticed that the met
banner is byte-identical at minute one and at hour twenty: `It looks like time.` above
`That meets the 5-1-1 guideline many providers use. Calling your provider now is
reasonable — their instructions come first.` It read as the app having nothing to say
to a woman who has been living inside the same screen all day.

## Decision

It stays identical. No copy is added that treats a long hold differently from a short
one, and no tone shifts with elapsed time.

## Rationale

**The figures beside it already age.** The banner's own sentence carries the holding
duration — "Averaging every 5:00, lasting 1:02, for 1 H 8 M" becomes "for 20 H 12 M" —
and the HOLDING leg carries it again. The two cases are not presented identically; the
*verdict clause* is identical and the evidence around it is not. Decision 0022's rule —
no figure in the present tense without something dating it — is already satisfied here.

**Every wording that would distinguish them is already banned.** Working through what
hour twenty could actually say:

- *"This has been going a long time"* — prognosis. The app does not know whether that
  is normal for her, and saying it implies it is not.
- *"Consider calling again"* — instruction. The app reports a pattern against the
  guideline she chose; her provider's instructions come first, and second-guessing them
  is the thing the voice rules forbid outright.
- *"Still meeting 5-1-1"* — true, and already what the screen says.
- Shading the tone at hour twenty — softer, graver, more concerned — is fear language
  by another route. Red is not in this palette and neither is worry.

**The verdict is the same verdict.** It is a statement about the measurement, not about
her endurance. A pattern that met the rule at minute one and still meets it at hour
twenty *is* the same finding; printing different words for the same finding would make
the screen less honest rather than more sympathetic.

**What was rejected, and the cost of being wrong.** The tempting middle path — one
extra sentence acknowledging a long hold without advising anything — was rejected
because no draft of it survived the list above: every version either implied a
prognosis or implied an action. The cost of being wrong is that a woman at hour twenty
feels unseen by a screen that is still technically accurate. That is a real cost. It is
smaller than the cost of an app that starts editorialising about how long her labor is
taking.

## Consequences

- F-0052 closes as "no change, recorded".
- This is **not** a claim that the long haul needs nothing. Decision 0041 already
  answers its sharpest case — the *irregular* early stretch, where silence read as
  something going wrong and a rest licence was the fix. That case had a real asymmetry
  to name: an intermittent sample of a slow, irregular pattern shows the same pattern,
  so watching continuously buys nothing. The held-met case has no equivalent fact to
  offer, which is exactly why it gets no equivalent sentence.
- What the long haul may genuinely lack is night-to-night vocabulary — F-0050, now
  HL-030 — which is a feature with storage and privacy consequences, not a sentence.
- Revisit on evidence from a woman who has lived it, not on a third reading of a
  screenshot at hour twenty.
